Slide 15
Welch spectral extraction
Welch PSD per channel: 100 Hz, 400-sample windows (4 seconds), 200-sample overlap.
Integrate each band with a lower-inclusive, upper-exclusive frequency mask.
Average band powers across the four channels before computing relative powers and ratios.
Relative power denominator: measured total power in 0.5–30 Hz. Aggregate epoch features by arithmetic mean per recording.

Slide 16
Relative powers and Delta ratios
Relative band power = mean channel band power / mean channel total power
Band/Delta ratio = mean channel band power / mean channel Delta power
Delta is a project feature-engineering reference, not a mandatory denominator.
Normalized EEG powers are spectral features; do not describe them as calibrated absolute physical power.

Sleep duration and efficiency
Each stored epoch contributes 0.5 minutes. Wake is excluded from TST.
Sleep efficiency uses the duration of the evaluated sequence, not independently verified time in bed.
N1/N2 refinement cannot change TST or efficiency because both labels are sleep.
SN001’s 91.5 minutes predicted sleep in a 95-minute segment must not be treated as whole-night sleep duration.

Slide 26
Sleep initiation and Wake after sleep onset
SOL = index of first non-Wake epoch × 0.5 minutes.
WASO includes every Wake epoch after onset, including Wake at the end of this notebook’s sequence.
REM latency = time from first sleep epoch to first REM epoch.
No sleep or no REM makes the corresponding metric unavailable. Segment onset is relative to the segment, not lights-out.

Slide 27
Stage percentages and fragmentation
N1%, N2%, N3%, REM% use TST as the denominator and sum to approximately 100%.
Awakenings count non-Wake → Wake transitions after first sleep.
Stage transitions count every adjacent pair whose labels differ.
Fragmentation = stage transitions / TST hours. This project-defined index is not EEG micro-arousal scoring.

Slide 32
Z-scores and P10/P90 regions
z = (recording value − reference mean) / reference SD
LOW: below P10; NORMAL: P10 through P90; HIGH: above P90.
Z-score measures magnitude and direction; percentile regions provide a categorical reference comparison.
NORMAL means within the dataset interval. LOW/HIGH do not mean medically bad/good. Equality at either boundary stays within reference.
